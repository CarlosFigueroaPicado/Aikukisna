**Subject:** Request for permission to use the *Kriol–Inglish Dikshineri* in a non-commercial language-learning app

Dear members of the National Kriol Council,

My name is [full name], and I lead **Aikukisna**, a free mobile app that helps people on the Caribbean
Coast of Nicaragua learn Miskito, Spanish, Nicaraguan Creole English (Kriol) and Standard English. The
app works offline, because many communities in the region have poor internet access.

For Miskito we have more than 33,000 documented words. For Kriol we have fewer than 400. We have
found almost no openly licensed material for Nicaraguan Kriol, and published studies describe it as
closely related to, and mutually intelligible with, Belize Kriol. The *Kriol–Inglish Dikshineri* is
the most complete reference we know of.

**What we are asking for.** We would like written permission to:

1. Use the entries of the *Kriol–Inglish Dikshineri* (2nd edition, 2024, or the 2009 edition) as a
   **draft** word list in the app's offline dictionary and translator.
2. Use those entries to help train a small on-device language model that suggests translations. The
   model and the app are free and non-commercial.

**How we would use it.**

- Every entry taken from the dictionary would be labelled in the app as *"Belize Kriol — National Kriol
  Council"* and shown with a full credit line.
- The entries would be marked as **pending validation**. They would only be shown as Nicaraguan Kriol
  after native speakers from Bluefields, Corn Island or Pearl Lagoon have reviewed them. We are
  proposing a validation agreement with URACCAN and BICU, the two universities of the region.
- We would not sell the data or the dictionary, and we would not publish the dictionary as a separate
  file.
- We would remove the material at any time if the Council asks us to.

We are happy to accept any conditions you consider appropriate, such as a specific credit line, a
limit on the number of entries, or a review of the app before publication. We would also be glad to
share back any corrections or Nicaraguan variants that speakers identify, if that is useful to the
Council.

Thank you for your time and for the Council's work in documenting Kriol. I look forward to your reply.
